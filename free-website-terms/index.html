Free site terms.

What's included, what it costs, who owns what, and what happens if you leave. Written to be read, not to hide things.

Last updated: August 2026

1. The short version

We design and build your website at no charge. Billing starts when the site goes live, and only once you've told us you're happy with it. From then on it's £30 a month and we host it, maintain it and make your changes. You buy and own your domain. There's no minimum term — but if you stop paying, the website comes offline, because the monthly fee is what pays for the build.

Everything below is the detail. If anything here isn't clear, just ask — we're happy to talk it through.

2. About these terms

These terms apply to the free site service provided by Weston Business Authority ("WBA", "we", "us") to you, the customer. They take effect when you ask us to start building your website. They sit alongside our general terms of service; where the two differ, these terms apply to the free site service.

3. What we provide

  • Design and build of a website for your business, at no cost to you
  • Written content for the pages, based on information you give us
  • Hosting, and an SSL certificate so your site shows as secure
  • A contact form, click-to-call and WhatsApp links, a map, opening hours, a photo gallery and social links as standard
  • Search-engine basics: page titles, descriptions and a sitemap
  • Ongoing changes and updates to your existing pages, and new pages as your business grows
  • Support by WhatsApp or email when you need something changed

The number of pages and the scope of the build are whatever we agree with you before we start. We'll be clear about it at the time.

4. What you provide

  • Information about your business, and prompt answers when we ask questions
  • Photographs and any logos or branding you want used (we can arrange photography as a paid extra)
  • Your own domain name, purchased in your name (see section 7)
  • Confirmation that everything you send us is lawful and yours to use

If we can't get what we need from you, we may not be able to finish the build. We'll chase once or twice, then leave it with you.

5. When billing starts

Nothing is payable while we're designing and building. We'll show you the site before it goes anywhere, and we'll keep changing it until you're happy with it.

Your first payment is due when the site goes live, which only happens once you've told us you're happy for it to. If you decide at that point that you don't want it, we don't put it live and you owe us nothing.

After that you're billed monthly. Changes and improvements are part of the fee — we don't quote for them individually.

6. Fees and billing

The fee is £30 per month, billed monthly in advance, starting when your site goes live. It covers hosting, maintenance, support and your ongoing changes.

Your price is protected. If we ever change what we charge, you keep your existing price for at least six months from the day we tell you, so nothing lands on your next bill unexpectedly and you have time to plan. You are free to cancel at any point if the new price does not suit.

If a payment fails, we'll let you know and give you a reasonable chance to fix it. If it stays unpaid, we may suspend or take down the website.

7. Your domain name

You buy and own your domain name directly, in your own name. It typically costs around £12 a year and it is not included in our monthly fee. This is deliberate: it means the domain is permanently yours, you control it, and you're never dependent on us to keep it.

We'll happily walk you through buying it and we'll connect it to your website for you. We're not responsible for your domain registrar, for renewal reminders, or for a domain lapsing because it wasn't renewed.

8. Business email

A website and a business email address are two separate things, and the free site service does not include email.

If you would like enquiries to reach you on your own domain (for example hello@yourbusiness.co.uk rather than a personal Gmail address), most domain registrars offer free forwarding — mail sent to your business address simply lands in the inbox you already use. We are happy to set this up for you as part of the build, at no charge.

A full mailbox — one you can also send and reply from, with storage and calendars — is a paid service from a provider such as Google Workspace or Microsoft 365. That account is bought in your name and billed to you directly, typically a few pounds per user per month. We can help you choose and set one up, but we do not provide, host or manage your email, and we are not responsible for its availability or for mail that fails to arrive.

9. Third-party services and accounts

Your website depends on services run by other companies — hosting, domain registration, form handling, email delivery and similar.

Where a service forms part of what we provide, we hold that account and manage it for you, and you do not need to sign up for anything or agree to anyone else's terms. That is deliberate: it keeps the service simple for you.

Where a service must be in your own name — your domain registrar, an email mailbox, or a payment processor if you take payments online — you enter into that agreement directly with the provider, and their terms and charges apply to you rather than to us. We can walk you through setting these up and we will happily sit on the phone while you do it, but we cannot accept another company's terms on your behalf, and we are not responsible for their service, pricing, uptime or decisions.

If a third-party provider changes its terms, raises its prices or withdraws a service in a way that affects your website, we will tell you and suggest the best alternative.

10. Who owns what

This one matters, so we'll be direct about it.

Yours, always: your domain name, the context of your company or service, your photographs, your logo and your branding. If you leave, you keep all of it, and we'll provide copies of your content on request.

Ours: the website itself — the underlying code, design system, templates and hosting setup we built it on. The website is provided to you as an ongoing service rather than as a product you buy outright. That's precisely what allows us to build it for free rather than charging you thousands up front.

If you would rather own the finished website outright and host it yourself, that's absolutely fine — just ask, and we'll quote you for a standard paid build instead.

11. Changes and updates

Your monthly fee includes reasonable ongoing changes: updating text, prices, opening hours, photos, staff, and adding new pages as you grow. We don't invoice you per change and we don't count them pettily.

"Reasonable" means what a normal small business needs. A complete redesign, an e-commerce shop, a booking or payment system, custom software, photography, or work for a separate business are all outside the monthly and would be quoted separately. We'll always tell you before anything is chargeable — you'll never receive a surprise invoice.

12. Cancelling

There is no minimum term and no notice period. You can cancel at any time by telling us in writing (WhatsApp or email is fine).

When you cancel: your subscription stops at the end of the period you've paid for, and your website is then taken offline. We keep your site files and content for 14 days afterwards, so there's a window to change your mind or ask us for a copy of your content. After 14 days they're deleted permanently.

Your domain is unaffected and remains yours, because you own it directly.

We may also end the service ourselves — for example if fees go unpaid, if the content becomes unlawful, or if the relationship becomes unworkable. We'll give you reasonable notice and the same 14-day window wherever we can.

13. Refunds

You see the finished site and approve it before anything is payable, so monthly payments are non-refundable once made — including part-months when you cancel partway through a billing period.

Nothing here affects any legal rights you have that can't be excluded.

We'll fix problems as quickly as we reasonably can.

Occasionally we need to take a site offline briefly for maintenance or updates. We'll keep it short and, where we can, do it quietly.
